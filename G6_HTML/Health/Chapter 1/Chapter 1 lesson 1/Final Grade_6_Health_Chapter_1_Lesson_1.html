Easy Lesson

What Is Health?

Teacher's exact definition
Health – The combination of physical, mental/emotional, and social well-being.

Looking fit and feeling well are important, but good health also includes getting along well with others and feeling good about yourself.

Physical+Mental/Emotional+Social=Total Health
⭐ Remember: Health is a combination of all three parts.
Easy Lesson

The Health Triangle

Think of good health as a triangle made of three equally-sized lines. Each line is one part of health.

Balanced

You think equally about all three sides.

Out of Balance

One side is longer because it receives more attention than the other two.

Goal

Maintain each part equally to stay healthy.

⭐ Remember: Equal sides = balanced total health.
Physical Health

Health of Your Body

Physical health is one side of the health triangle. If you feel strong and have lots of energy, you probably have good physical health.

Eat healthy foods
Choose nutritious meals and snacks.
Visit the doctor regularly
Get doctor and dentist checkups.
Care for personal hygiene
Bathe daily; brush and floss at least twice daily.
Wear protective gear
Follow safety rules to avoid injuries.
Get enough sleep
Most teens need about nine hours nightly.
Be physically active
Daily activity builds and maintains physical health.
⭐ Remember: Body care needs food, activity, checkups, hygiene, safety, and sleep.
Behavior

Actions and Choices

Teacher's exact definition
Behavior – The way you act in many different situations and events in your life.

Good physical health includes avoiding harmful behavior such as using alcohol, tobacco, and other drugs.

Smoking or tobacco can harm the mouth, heart, and lungs. Alcohol and other drug use can damage the liver, brain, and other organs.

⭐ Remember: Behavior means the way you act.
Cause → Choice

Internal Factors

Internal factors can influence behavior about tobacco, alcohol, and other drugs.

Curious about smoking→Might try it
Afraid of drug effects→May never use drugs
Interests place you near use→Use decision-making skills
⭐ Remember: Internal factors can influence choices; use decision-making skills to avoid harmful behaviors.
Mental and Emotional Health

Mind and Emotions

This side involves how you handle your feelings, thoughts, and emotions.

  • Talk and think about yourself in a healthful way.
  • Share thoughts and feelings with family, a trusted adult, or a friend.
  • Face challenges positively and be patient while learning.
  • Remember that everybody makes mistakes; next time you can do better.
  • Take action toward goals to focus energy and feel accomplishment.
  • Make healthful choices, keep promises, and take responsibility.
⭐ Remember: Healthy thinking helps you face feelings, mistakes, challenges, and goals.
Social Health

Getting Along with People

Teacher's exact definition
Social Health – The ability to get along with people around you.

It includes the health of friendships and how you relate to people at home, at school, and everywhere in your world.

  • Support people you care about.
  • Communicate with, respect, and value people.
  • During disagreement, express thoughts thoughtfully and choose words carefully.
⭐ Remember: Social health grows through support, communication, respect, and careful words.
Achieving Wellness

Health and Wellness

Teacher's exact definition
Wellness – State of wellbeing or balanced health over a long period of time.
IdeaMeaning in the material
HealthLike a snapshot of physical, mental/emotional, and social health; it can change from day to day.
WellnessTakes a longer view; it balances the three sides over weeks or months.
⭐ Remember: Health = snapshot; wellness = longer view.
Habits

Patterns You Follow

Teacher's exact definition
Habits – Patterns of behavior that you follow almost without thinking.

Good health habits in the material include:

Eating healthy foods
60 minutes of physical activity on most days
Managing stress
Developing healthy relationships
⭐ Remember: Repeated good habits can improve wellness over time.
Wellness Scale

From Poor to Peak Health

Wellness can be shown as a continuum, or scale, giving a picture of health at a certain time and how well you are taking care of yourself.

Poor Health→Average Health→Peak Health
Toward peak health
Practices good habits and behaviors; stays informed.
Toward poor health
Practices unhealthy habits and behaviors; lacks up-to-date information.
⭐ Remember: The scale shows health from poor → average → peak.
Mind and Body

The Mind-Body Connection

Teacher's exact definition
Mind-body Connection – How your emotions affect your physical and over-all health and how your over-all health affects your emotions.

The nervous system connects mind and body. Nerves link the brain to the body. Upsetting thoughts and feelings sometimes affect signals from the brain to other body parts.

⭐ Remember: Emotions ↔ physical and overall health.
Cause → Effect

Strong Emotions and Health

Difficult emotions→Negative physical effect

The material gives examples such as headaches, backaches, upset stomachs, colds, the flu, or more serious diseases.

If you become very sad or angry, or have other strong emotions, talk to someone. A good friend may help. Sometimes a counselor or medical professional may be needed.

⭐ Remember: When strong emotions are hard to handle, talk to someone.
Myth vs. Fact

Mental Illness

MythFact from the material
Poor upbringing or lack of willpower causes mental illness.Mental illnesses can be caused by some of the same factors as physical illnesses.

The material gives this example: type 1 diabetes is caused by chemical imbalances in the body, and depression is believed to be caused by chemical imbalances in the brain.

⭐ Remember: The material says mental illness is not simply poor upbringing or lack of willpower.
Environment

Where You Live and Who Is Around You

Physical environmentSocial environment
Where you live: home, school, air, and water around you.People in your life: friends, classmates, and neighbors; recreation is also part of it.

Peers are people close to you in age who are a lot like you. Friends and peers may influence your choices.

⭐ Remember: Physical environment is about places and surroundings; social environment is about people and recreation.
Peer Influence

Positive or Negative

You may feel pressure to think and act like your peers. Peer pressure can also influence healthful choices.

Positive influence

Helping with homework, volunteering together, listening to a friend, playing games, or enjoying physical activities with others.

Negative influence

A friend wants you to drink alcohol.

⭐ Remember: Peer influence can be positive or negative.
Final 2-Minute Review

Remember These Before the Test

  • Health combines physical + mental/emotional + social well-being.
  • The health triangle should have three balanced sides.
  • Physical health cares for the body; mental/emotional health cares for mind and emotions; social health is getting along with people.
  • Health is a snapshot; wellness is balanced health over a long period.
  • Habits are patterns followed almost without thinking.
  • The mind-body connection works in both directions.
  • Environment can be physical or social; peer influence can be positive or negative.
⭐ Remember: Balance the triangle, build good habits, and understand the connections.
