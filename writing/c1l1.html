Build Clear, Correct, and Interesting Sentences

Learn sentence kinds, punctuation, capital letters, sentence building, varied beginnings, and sentence transformation.

Learn→See examples→Practice
⭐ Remember: Clear writing begins with a clear sentence.

1. What You Must Know

Sentence purpose

Declarative, interrogative, imperative, and exclamatory

Writing marks

Punctuation and capital letters

Sentence structure

Simple, compound, and complex

Sentence variety

Start with time, place, action, feeling, dialogue, condition, emphasis, comparison, or summary.

⭐ Remember: Know the purpose, marks, structure, and beginning of each sentence.

Four Kinds of Sentences

KindWhat it doesExample
DeclarativeTells something.The puppy chased the ball.
InterrogativeAsks something.Did the puppy chase the ball?
ImperativeGives a direction or command.Chase the ball.
ExclamatoryShows strong feeling.What an exciting chase!
⭐ Remember: Tell, ask, direct, or show strong feeling.

Declarative vs. Interrogative

Declarative

It tells.

Maya opened the window.
Interrogative

It asks.

Did Maya open the window?
Tell→.Ask→?
⭐ Remember: A question needs a question mark.

Imperative vs. Exclamatory

Imperative

It gives a direction or command.

Open the window.
Exclamatory

It shows a strong feeling.

What a strong wind!
⭐ Remember: Imperative directs; exclamatory expresses strong feeling.

Punctuation Gives the Reader Signals

Pause→Stop→Ask→Shout→Hear

Punctuation tells the reader when to pause, stop, ask, shout, or hear.

One small mark can change how a sentence sounds.
⭐ Remember: Punctuation is a road sign for the reader.

Period, Question Mark, Exclamation Mark

Period .

Stops a telling sentence.

The rain stopped.
Question Mark ?

Ends a question.

Did the rain stop?
Exclamation Mark !

Shows a shout or strong feeling.

The rain stopped!
⭐ Remember: Tell = . Ask = ? Strong feeling = !

Comma and Apostrophe

Comma ,

Shows a pause.

After that, I enjoyed a yummy ice cream.
Apostrophe '

Can show that something belongs to someone.

Maya's bag is blue.
⭐ Remember: Comma = pause; apostrophe = belonging in this example.

Colon, Semicolon, Quotation Marks

MarkWhat it doesEasy example
Colon :Introduces a list, explanation, or example.I need three things: paper, pens, and a notebook.
Semicolon ;Joins two related independent clauses.I wanted to go out; it started to rain.
Quotation marks “ ”Show words that we hear someone say.Maya said, “Open the window.”
⭐ Remember: Colon introduces; semicolon joins; quotation marks show spoken words.

Capital Letters

Use a capital for...Example
The first word of a sentenceThe puppy chased the ball.
The name of a personMaya opened the window.
A place or countryMaya lives in Thailand.
Days and monthsWe study on Monday in June.
The word II studied for the test.
Titles when neededDr. Lee opened the door.
⭐ Remember: Start, names, places, countries, days, months, I, and needed titles.

Three Ways to Build a Sentence

Simple→Compound→Complex

Simple

One complete idea.

Compound

Two complete ideas joined by FANBOYS.

Complex

One main idea + one dependent idea.

⭐ Remember: Count complete ideas and look for joining words.

Simple Sentence

One complete idea→Simple
The students laughed.
The dragon slept beside the castle.

A simple sentence can begin in an interesting way and still stay simple.

Yesterday, I went to the beach.
⭐ Remember: Simple means one complete idea.

Compound Sentence

Complete idea, + FANBOYSComplete idea

FANBOYS = for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so

I was tired, but I finished my homework.
The rain stopped, so we went outside.
⭐ Remember: Compound = two complete ideas joined by FANBOYS.

Complex Sentence

Main idea+Dependent idea

Words from the material: because, although, when, while, if, after, before.

After the bell rang, we packed our bags.
Because I studied for the test, I felt confident.
⭐ Remember: Complex = one main idea and one dependent idea.

Easy-to-Confuse Points

SimpleCompoundComplex
One complete ideaTwo complete ideasOne main idea + one dependent idea
The students laughed.I wanted to play soccer, but I had to finish my homework.Although the path was steep, we continued climbing.
⭐ Remember: Compound has two complete ideas; complex has a dependent idea.

Try Different Sentence Beginnings

Time

Yesterday, I went to the beach.

Place

Beside the castle, the dragon slept.

Action

Running quickly, the puppy chased the ball.

Feeling

Feeling confident, I took the test.

Dialogue

“Let’s go outside,” Maya said.

Condition

If the rain stops, we will go outside.

Emphasis

Most importantly, finish your homework.

Comparison

Unlike the quiet room, the classroom was loud.

Summary

In summary, varied beginnings make writing interesting.

⭐ Remember: Change the beginning to make writing more interesting.

Variety Makes Writing More Interesting

Boring: same beginning

I went to the beach.
I built a sandcastle.
I ate ice cream.

Better: varied beginnings

Yesterday, I went to the beach.
Near the water, I built a huge sandcastle.
After that, I enjoyed yummy ice cream.

Writing note: “Build” was changed to “built” because the other actions happened in the past.
⭐ Remember: Do not start every sentence the same way.

How to Transform a Sentence

Step 1: Find the idea→Step 2: Choose the target→Step 3: Join or reduce
  • For compound, write two complete ideas and join them with FANBOYS.
  • For complex, write one main idea and one dependent idea.
  • For simple, keep one complete idea.
⭐ Remember: Keep the main meaning while changing the structure.

Transforming Sentence 1

Question

The puppy chased the ball.

Change it to a COMPOUND sentence.

Answer

The puppy chased the ball, and it rolled across the yard.

Explanation

It has two complete ideas joined by the FANBOYS word and.

⭐ Remember: Compound = complete idea + FANBOYS + complete idea.

Transforming Sentence 2

Question

Maya opened the window.

Change it to a COMPLEX sentence.

Answer

When Maya opened the window, fresh air entered the room.

Explanation

When Maya opened the window is dependent; fresh air entered the room is the main idea.

⭐ Remember: Complex = dependent idea + main idea.

Transforming Sentence 3

Question

The rain stopped, so we went outside.

Change it to a SIMPLE sentence.

Answer

After the rain, we went outside.

Explanation

Only one complete idea remains: we went outside.

⭐ Remember: A beginning phrase can still be part of a simple sentence.

Transforming Sentence 4

Question

Because I studied for the test, I felt confident.

Change it to a COMPOUND sentence.

Answer

I studied for the test, so I felt confident.

Explanation

It has two complete ideas joined by the FANBOYS word so.

⭐ Remember: “So” joins a cause to its result.

Transforming Sentence 5

Question

The students laughed.

Change it to a COMPOUND sentence.

Answer

The students laughed, and everyone felt happy.

Explanation

It has two complete ideas joined by the FANBOYS word and.

⭐ Remember: Both sides of a compound sentence must be complete ideas.

Transforming Sentence 6

Question

I wanted to play soccer, but I had to finish my homework.

Change it to a COMPLEX sentence.

Answer

Although I wanted to play soccer, I had to finish my homework.

Explanation

Although I wanted to play soccer is dependent; the other part is the main idea.

⭐ Remember: “Although” can begin a dependent idea that shows contrast.

Transforming Sentence 7

Question

When the bell rang, everyone rushed out of the classroom.

Change it to a SIMPLE sentence.

Answer

At the bell, everyone rushed out of the classroom.

Explanation

Only one complete idea remains: everyone rushed out of the classroom.

⭐ Remember: Change the dependent idea into a short beginning phrase.

Transforming Sentence 8

Question

The dragon slept beside the castle.

Change it to a COMPLEX sentence.

Answer

While the dragon slept beside the castle, everything was quiet.

Explanation

The idea beginning with while is dependent; everything was quiet is the main idea.

⭐ Remember: “While” can add a dependent time idea.

Transforming Sentence 9

Question

The lights went out, and the room became completely silent.

Change it to a SIMPLE sentence.

Answer

The dark room became completely silent.

Explanation

The new sentence has one complete idea.

⭐ Remember: A simple sentence keeps one complete idea.

Transforming Sentence 10

Question

Although the path was steep, we continued climbing.

Change it to a COMPOUND sentence.

Answer

The path was steep, but we continued climbing.

Explanation

It has two complete ideas joined by the FANBOYS word but.

⭐ Remember: “But” joins two complete ideas that contrast.

Exam Focus

  • Identify the four kinds of sentences.
  • Choose the correct end punctuation.
  • Recognize the common punctuation marks named in the material.
  • Fix capital letters.
  • Tell simple, compound, and complex sentences apart.
  • Use FANBOYS in a compound sentence.
  • Recognize because, although, when, while, if, after, and before.
  • Transform a sentence without losing its main meaning.
  • Use varied sentence beginnings.
⭐ Remember: Expect to identify, correct, choose, and transform.
