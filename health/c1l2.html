Must-Know Vocabulary 1

Culture and Cultural Background

Teacher's exact definition
CULTURE
Simple meaning: The collected beliefs, customs, and behaviors of a group.
What it really means: A group’s shared ways can influence foods, activities, celebrations, and behavior.
Easy example: Ahmed’s family fasts until sundown during Ramadan.
Remember: Culture = a GROUP’S shared ways.
Teacher's exact definition
CULTURAL BACKGROUND
Simple meaning: The beliefs, customs, and traditions of a specific group of people.
What it really means: Your family helps shape it.
Easy example: Family traditions may include dances, foods, ceremonies, songs, and games.
Remember: Cultural background = beliefs + customs + traditions.
⭐ Remember: Culture is shared by a group; your family shapes your cultural background.
Must-Know Vocabulary 2

Heredity

Teacher's exact definition
HEREDITY
Simple meaning: The passing of traits from parents to their biological children.
What it really means: Biological parents pass traits to children; not all inherited traits can be controlled.
Easy example: Natural hair color or eye color.
Remember: Heredity = traits PASSED from biological parents.

You receive half of your DNA from your mother and half from your father. Family history can help identify illnesses and diseases for which you might be at risk. In some cases, you can act to reduce risk.

⭐ Remember: Family history can reveal possible health risks.
Must-Know Vocabulary 3

Chromosomes and Genes

Teacher's exact definition
CHROMOSOMES
Simple meaning: Threadlike structures that carry genes.
What it really means: Each human has 46 chromosomes; each parent contributes 23 to a child.
Easy example: A child receives chromosomes from both parents.
Remember: Chromosomes CARRY genes.
Teacher's exact definition
GENES
Simple meaning: The basic unit of heredity.
What it really means: Genetic information such as hair and eye color is stored in genes. Genes are made from chemicals called DNA.
Easy example: Siblings may inherit different chromosomes and look or act differently.
Remember: Genes STORE genetic information.
⭐ Remember: 46 total = 23 from each parent; chromosomes carry genes.
Must-Know Vocabulary 4

Media

Teacher's exact definition
MEDIA
Simple meaning: Various methods for communication information.
What it really means: Television, radio, movies, magazines, newspapers, books, billboards, and the Internet can influence beliefs, actions, appearance ideas, and products you want.
Easy example: An ad may tell you about a community health program or encourage an unhealthful eating plan.
Remember: Media = many ways information REACHES you.

Online health information may come from unreliable sources and may not be accurate. Evaluate the quality of everything you see, hear, or read.

⭐ Remember: Media can help or harm; evaluate its quality.
Must-Know Vocabulary 5

Lifestyle Factors and Attitude

Teacher's exact definition
LIFESTYLE FACTORS
Simple meaning: Behaviors and habits that help determine a person’s level of health.
What it really means: Positive factors promote good health; negative factors promote poor health.
Easy example: Getting enough sleep is a positive lifestyle factor.
Remember: Lifestyle factors = HABITS that affect health.
Teacher's exact definition
ATTITUDE
Simple meaning: A personal feeling or belief.
What it really means: A positive attitude makes teens more likely to practice good health habits and take responsibility.
Easy example: Believing that caring for your health is important.
Remember: Attitude = personal FEELING or BELIEF.
⭐ Remember: Positive lifestyle factors and a positive attitude support good health.
Must-Know Vocabulary 6

Risk and Sedentary Lifestyle

Teacher's exact definition
RISK
Simple meaning: The chance that something harmful may happen to your health and wellness.
What it really means: Some risks are easy to see; others are hidden. Every day includes some risk.
Easy example: Preparing food with a knife or crossing a busy street.
Remember: Risk = CHANCE of harm.
Teacher's exact definition
SEDENTARY LIFESTYLE
Simple meaning: A way of life that involves little physical activity.
What it really means: It is a risk behavior that can be avoided.
Easy example: Choosing little physical activity as a way of life.
Remember: Sedentary = LITTLE activity.
⭐ Remember: Risk is a chance of harm; sedentary means little activity.
Must-Know Vocabulary 7

Cumulative Risk

Teacher's exact definition
CUMULATIVE RISK
Simple meaning: When one risk factor adds to another to increase danger.
What it really means: Risks combine over time and raise the total danger.
Easy example: Unhealthy food choices + no regular physical activity → higher risk of heart disease and cancer over time.
Remember: Cumulative risk = risks ADD UP.
Risk 1+Risk 2→Greater Risk
⭐ Remember: One risk + another risk = increased danger.
Must-Know Vocabulary 8

Prevention and Precautions

Teacher's exact definition
PREVENTION
Simple meaning: Taking steps to avoid something.
What it really means: Watch for dangers and act to avoid injury or accidents.
Easy example: Wear a helmet when riding a bike or slow down on wet or icy pavement.
Remember: Prevention = take steps to AVOID.
Defined in the lesson
PRECAUTIONS
Simple meaning: Planned actions taken before an event to increase the chances of a safe outcome.
What it really means: Plan before danger happens.
Easy example: Wearing protective gear before playing a sport.
Remember: Precaution = PLAN BEFORE.
⭐ Remember: Prevention avoids danger; precautions are planned before an event.
Must-Know Vocabulary 9

Abstinence

Teacher's exact definition
ABSTINENCE
Simple meaning: The conscious active choice not to participate in high risk behavior.
What it really means: You actively choose not to take part in high-risk behaviors to prevent illness, injury, and negative consequences.
Easy example: Choosing not to smoke and staying away from alcohol, illegal drugs, and sexual activity.
Remember: Abstinence = actively CHOOSE NOT TO.

It supports physical health, gives peace of mind, benefits relationships, and shows responsibility and respect for yourself and others.

⭐ Remember: Abstinence can support all three sides of the health triangle.
Other Defined Concepts

Addictions and Self-Discipline

Defined in the lesson
ADDICTIONS
Simple meaning: Physical and mental dependencies.
What it really means: Alcohol, tobacco, or other drugs can lead to dangerous physical and mental dependencies.
Easy example: Experimenting with these substances can lead to addiction.
Remember: Addictions = physical + mental DEPENDENCIES.
Explained in the lesson
SELF-DISCIPLINE
Simple meaning: Commitment to developing a healthy lifestyle.
What it really means: Remind yourself to make the healthful choice.
Easy example: Turn off the TV or computer and take part in activities that build physical health.
Remember: Self-discipline = remind yourself and ACT.
⭐ Remember: Avoid dependencies and use commitment to build a healthy lifestyle.
Most Important Lesson Ideas

What Influences Health?

You can control some, but not all, health influences.

Influences named in the lesson

heredityenvironmentfamily and friendsculturemediaattitudesbehavior

Why it matters

These factors can influence you to make healthful or unhealthful choices.

Example: Heredity affects natural hair or eye color, while behavior includes choices you make.

⭐ Remember: Some influences are not controllable, but many behaviors and choices are.
Most Important Lesson Ideas

Culture and Media Influence Choices

Culture

May influence food, activities, special events, and how people behave toward each other.

Example: A culture that encourages a healthy diet and exercise.

Media

May influence actions, beliefs, ideas about appearance, and products you think you should own.

Example: Ads may promote a health program or an unhealthful eating plan.

Why it matters: Learn to evaluate everything you see, hear, or read because online sources may be unreliable or inaccurate.

⭐ Remember: Culture and media can influence health in helpful or harmful ways.
Important List

Positive Lifestyle Factors

Eat well-balanced meals, starting with a good breakfast.
Get at least 60 minutes of physical activity daily.
Sleep at least eight hours every night.
Do your best in school and other activities.
Avoid tobacco, alcohol, and other drugs.
Follow safety rules and wear protective gear.
Relate well to family, friends, and classmates.
Have a trusted adult to talk to when you have questions or a problem.
⭐ Remember: Positive lifestyle factors promote good health.
Risks and Consequences

Short-Term or Long-Term

TypeLesson example
Minor or short-termEating a sweet snack before dinner may make you lose your appetite for a healthy meal.
Serious or long-termAlcohol, tobacco, or other drugs can seriously damage health, affect all three health-triangle sides, lead to addictions, confuse judgment, increase risks, and cause family, friend, or school problems.

Knowing the consequences of choices and behaviors can help you take responsibility for your health.

⭐ Remember: Every risk behavior has a consequence, which may be short-term or long-term.
Reducing Risks

Four Ways to Lower Danger

1 Prevention

Take steps to avoid something.

2 Precautions

Plan actions before an event for a safer outcome.

3 Stay Informed

Learn about health developments and get checked by a doctor.

4 Choose Abstinence

Actively choose not to participate in high-risk behaviors.

See possible danger→Choose a safe action→Reduce risk
⭐ Remember: The material says prevention is the best way to deal with risks.
Important Comparisons

Do Not Mix These Up

IdeasEasy difference
Heredity vs behaviorHeredity passes traits from biological parents; behavior involves the way you act and choices you make.
Culture vs cultural backgroundCulture is a group’s collected beliefs, customs, and behaviors; cultural background is the beliefs, customs, and traditions of a specific group shaped strongly by family.
Risk vs cumulative riskRisk is a chance of harm; cumulative risk happens when risks add together and increase danger.
Prevention vs precautionPrevention means taking steps to avoid something; a precaution is a planned action taken before an event.
Positive vs negative lifestyle factorsPositive factors promote good health; negative factors promote poor health.
⭐ Remember: Compare the meaning and example of each idea.
